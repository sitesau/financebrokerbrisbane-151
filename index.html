Preparing a Better Broker Brief

The enquiry process asks for your financing needs, the approximate amount, and the desired timing within Greater Brisbane. Before submitting, it is advisable to draft a concise brief. This brief should clearly state the loan's purpose, the property's location, your current stage in the process, and any documents you already possess, such as a current loan statement, a preliminary budget, or a purchase contract. ASIC's Moneysmart advises that comparing home loans involves examining interest rates, fees, features, and repayment terms. When speaking with brokers, ask how they will explain these aspects, whether features like offset or redraw accounts are relevant to your situation, and what potential costs you might incur. It is important to keep lending-related questions separate from tax planning decisions. For investment loans, a broker can advise on credit options, but matters like negative gearing, ownership structure, and tax deductibility should be discussed with a qualified tax adviser. The initial call should focus on confirming the broker's process, not on making every decision.

Checks Before Sharing Application Documents

The enquiry process is described as free and non-obligatory, which can facilitate initial contact. However, sensitive financial documents should only be shared once you understand who is requesting them and the purpose. ASIC regulates financial services and credit activities in Australia. Before proceeding, inquire about the broker's licence or representative status, their fee structure, commission arrangements, the panel of lenders they work with, their complaint handling procedures, and how any recommendations will be documented. For a fair comparison, provide consistent information to each shortlisted broker. Share the same loan purpose, approximate amount, timing, and property area. Ask what further information they require, their process for comparing lenders, and if there are any upfront costs before an application is submitted.
